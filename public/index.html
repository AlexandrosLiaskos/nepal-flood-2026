2026 Nepal Debris Avalanche and Flash Flood

Niki Evelpidou1, Alexandros Liaskos1, Evaggelos Spyrou1, and Nitesh Khadka2

1 National and Kapodistrian University of Athens

2 Centre for Water Resources Studies, Institute of Engineering, Tribhuvan University, Nepal

On 26 August 2026, a glacier-ice detachment near the Nepal–China border generated a debris avalanche and rapidly propagating flood along the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli river system. The downstream propagation pathway was delineated by integrating satellite-derived flow extents and geolocated damage observations from the United Nations Satellite Centre (UNOSAT) and the Copernicus Emergency Management Service (CEMS) with Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT) mapping, and its longitudinal profile was reconstructed using the NASA High Mountain Asia and Copernicus digital elevation models.

Source-event reconstruction

The lower part of a glacier near Langtang Lirung detached and fell hundreds of metres onto the valley floor. Satellite imagery shows a large section missing between 24 and 26 August 2026, followed by an ice-and-rock avalanche entering the Lhende Khola river system.

  • University of Calgary geomorphologist Dan Shugar rapidly located the collapse source using Planet Labs imagery and publicly identified the flood as originating from a collapsed glacier, supporting an ice–rock collapse interpretation rather than a glacial lake outburst flood. Source · Interview
  • The U.S. Geological Survey catalogues the 02:52:10 UTC seismic signal as an M5.2 landslide and states that the glacial collapse generated energy equivalent to an M5.2 earthquake; it was not a tectonic earthquake. Source
  • The estimated debris-flow velocity was approximately 50 m s−1, with an affected runout exceeding 20 km.

Source

Bridge observation

China Central Television (CCTV) security-camera footage, originally posted by Annapurna, records the first observed arrival of the debris flood at the bridge at 11:00:00 China Standard Time on 26 August 2026, equivalent to 03:00:00 UTC and 08:45:00 Nepal Time.

From the 02:52:10 UTC seismic onset to the recorded arrival, the elapsed time is 7 min 50 s. Applying this interval to the reconstructed valley-path distance implies a potential path-averaged propagation speed of 49.6–50.8 m s−1. This is a kinematic estimate, not a direct measurement of instantaneous debris-flow velocity; debris volume, entrainment, rheology, channel geometry, temporary storage, and other controlling parameters are not constrained.

      About this Project

      With respect and solidarity

      We extend our deepest sympathy to the people and communities affected by the 26 August 2026 disaster, especially to those who lost family members and loved ones, sustained injuries, were displaced, or suffered the loss of homes, livelihoods, infrastructure, and cultural or natural heritage. We stand in solidarity with Nepal and with every community along the affected region.

      This research is offered in the hope that it can strengthen scientific understanding, risk awareness, preparedness, and future efforts to prevent or mitigate such hazards and catastrophes.
